# Fellowship Operating System

This document turns the fellowship promise into a repeatable operating rhythm while preserving Code Orange's Southeast Asia priority and privacy/wallet infrastructure focus.

## 1. Capacity Comes Before Recruitment

Do not accept a fellow until one primary mentor, one fallback contact, and a project-fit work plan are recorded. Start the first cohort with no more seats than this table supports.

| Track | Mentor | Project / area | Maximum fellows | Confirmed fellows | Fallback contact | Weekly slot | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Developer | `unassigned` | Privacy / wallet infrastructure | 0 | 0 | `unassigned` | `unassigned` | Not ready to recruit |
| Educator | `unassigned` | Southeast Asia cohort delivery | 0 | 0 | `unassigned` | `unassigned` | Not ready to recruit |

**Capacity rule:** one mentor supports at most two developer fellows and must commit to a prepared weekly 30-60 minute check-in. Upstream maintainers are never promised a review SLA; Code Orange supplies structure, escalation, and peer review while the upstream project controls acceptance.

## 2. Eligibility and Selection

### Developer fellowship minimum eligibility

- Southeast Asia-based applicants receive priority; remote applicants need a strong underrepresented-community or project-fit case.
- Completion of a Code Orange technical cohort or equivalent evidence of Bitcoin development study.
- A reproducible local development environment and a scoped six-month plan for a Bitcoin OSS project.
- At least one upstream PR submitted by the fellowship start date. An application may be reviewed provisionally before that point, but no seat is awarded without the evidence.
- A named mentor fit and, where possible, a project maintainer or contributor endorsement.
- Realistic availability: 15-20 hours per week part-time or 35+ hours full-time.

### Selection rubric (100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Evidence |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Upstream contribution evidence and code-reading ability | 30 | Linked PRs, reviews, issues, test work, or a walkthrough of the target codebase |
| Project fit and public value | 25 | Priority for privacy, wallet, and related Bitcoin infrastructure; a concrete user or protocol problem |
| Six-month scope and milestones | 20 | Deliverables sized for upstream feedback rather than a PR-count target |
| Mentor and maintainer fit | 15 | Confirmed primary mentor, fallback, and relevant project relationship |
| Community reciprocity and regional impact | 10 | Peer support, workshop help, documentation, or a credible Southeast Asia knowledge-sharing plan |

Record the evidence, total, decision, and reviewer in the cohort record. Do not select on promise alone.

## 3. Evidence of Progress

| Rhythm | Fellow supplies | Mentor / program supplies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekly | One public link or reproducible artifact, blocker, and next action | Check-in note and an explicit unblock or escalation |
| Bi-weekly | Short peer demo or written progress update | Cohort feedback and connection to another contributor where useful |
| Monthly | Existing [monthly report](templates/monthly-report.md): PRs, reviews, issues, outcomes, blockers, next month | Review against the agreed work plan; update status green / yellow / red |

Count meaningful upstream work: tests, benchmarks, design discussion, code review, issues, and PRs. Merges are important lagging evidence, but should not create pressure for low-value PR volume.

## 4. Month-3 Continuation Review

Continue, adapt, or end the fellowship in a documented review. The fellow, mentor, and Fellowship Lead should answer:

- Were at least 80% of weekly check-ins completed with evidence?
- Is the original project still a good fit, and is upstream contact real?
- Has the fellow produced useful upstream evidence: reviews, tests, issue work, open PRs, or merged work?
- Is the work plan appropriately scoped for the remaining three months?
- Does the fellow have a clear next action, and does the mentor have capacity to continue?

**Decision rules:** green continues; yellow continues only with a written 30-day recovery plan; red pauses or exits with a respectful handoff. A delayed upstream merge alone is not a red signal.

## 5. First-Pilot Success Metrics

Measure the pilot as a system, not as a contest for the largest PR count:

| Metric | Pilot target |
| --- | ---: |
| Fellows with mentor and fallback confirmed before start | 100% |
| Scheduled weekly check-ins completed | 85%+ |
| Developer fellows submitting at least two upstream PRs | 80%+ |
| Developer fellows with one merged or maintainer-accepted contribution | 60%+ |
| Fellows completing the alumni handoff | 75%+ |
| Fellows submitting a grant or paid-FOSS application within 30 days | 50%+ |
| Fellows still making upstream activity after 90 days | 40%+ |

For educator fellows, also record at least 10 delivered sessions, participant second-session return, contribution evidence from participants, and one trained successor. Targets should be adjusted only with a dated cohort decision, not retroactively.

## 6. Alumni Handoff (starts in Month 5)

Every graduating fellow leaves with an owner and these artifacts:

- A public portfolio: linked contributions, concise project narrative, and impact or technical write-up.
- A grant-ready CV and a one-page funding narrative grounded in the fellow's actual work.
- A target list for OpenSats, HRF, Spiral, relevant employers, or project-specific funding, plus requested warm introductions where appropriate.
- A maintainer-reference request and a clearly defined next upstream contribution.
- A 30/90/180-day follow-up record owned by the Fellowship Lead or named alumni owner.

Graduation is a transition to independent contribution, not the end of contact. Alumni can return as peer reviewers, mentors, or educators only when that supports their own upstream work.
