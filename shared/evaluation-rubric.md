# Fellowship Evaluation Rubric

Used at the Month-3 review and graduation conversation. It makes expectations visible; it is not a stack rank, a PR contest, or a reason to surprise someone with a decision.

## Principles

- **Trajectory over starting level.** We assess whether a fellow is building capability and good working habits.
- **Evidence over activity.** A reliable artifact, learning outcome, or honest diagnosis matters more than a big number.
- **Context matters.** Upstream projects, languages, communities, travel, safety, and life circumstances differ.
- **Honesty helps.** A clearly reported bad month is a chance to adjust; prolonged silence makes support impossible.
- **No surprises.** Concerns are named when they emerge and have an owner, a support action, and a next review date.

## Developer track

| Area | Meeting expectations looks like | Strong progress looks like | Needs attention when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contribution practice | A steady loop of code reading, testing, issue work, PRs/patches, or documentation appropriate to the project | Takes responsibility for a meaningful problem or trusted area | No visible or reproducible work and no explanation |
| Review and collaboration | Gives or seeks specific feedback; responds constructively to project norms | Review helps others and demonstrates deep understanding | Only asks for review or repeats feedback without adaptation |
| Technical understanding | Can explain the problem, constraints, and what changed | Produces a clear guide, benchmark, or architecture note useful to others | Reporting remains vague or cannot explain work completed |
| Reliability and conduct | Weekly/monthly rhythm is dependable; blockers surface early | Peers and maintainers can rely on the fellow’s communication | Repeated unexplained absence or harmful collaboration |

## Educator track

| Area | Meeting expectations looks like | Strong progress looks like | Needs attention when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Delivery | A sustainable rhythm that moves toward the 24-credit quarterly target | A rhythm participants can plan around, with quality not sacrificed for credits | Sessions stop without communication or records are reconstructed much later |
| Participant outcomes | Evidence that learners return, complete a track, build capability, or take a next step | Participants begin helping peers or facilitating themselves | Attendance is the only measure and no learning is examined |
| Teaching craft | Specific reflection, preparation, and adaptation to feedback | A tested improvement or local-language approach others can reuse | Repeats a format that is not serving participants without adjustment |
| Commons and care | Materials are reusable where safe; privacy and community care are respected | A successor, translation, runbook, or peer network extends the work | Participant information is exposed or community concerns go unaddressed |

## Review outcomes

| Outcome | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Continue | The plan is working and support/capacity remain in place. |
| Continue with adjustment | A written 30-day scope, support, or rhythm change makes the work more viable. |
| Pause | The fellow needs time away; no stigma and no stipend during the agreed pause. |
| Graduate | The fellow packages evidence, next contribution, and funding/work/community path. |
| Conclude | The fellowship is no longer the right vehicle after direct conversation and a respectful handoff. |

Immediate action is reserved for a serious [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) concern. Otherwise, the sequence is: name the concern, talk about it, agree a change, review the change, then decide.
