# Fellow Handbook

This is the day-to-day guide for an accepted Code Orange fellow. It applies after your agreement and start date are confirmed.

## Your six-month rhythm

| When | Your responsibility | Program responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Before the start | Agree a scoped work plan, communication channel, and safe reporting arrangement. | Confirm mentor or program lead, fallback contact, payment process, and schedule. |
| Every week | Send a short [weekly update](templates/weekly-update.md); flag blockers early. | Hold or reschedule the check-in; offer a clear unblock, connection, or next action. |
| Every two weeks | Join a peer demo or share a written update where possible. | Create a useful forum for feedback and peer learning. |
| By the 5th | Send one [monthly update](templates/monthly-report.md). Educators also keep a [session log](educator-fellowship/session-log-template.md) within 48 hours. | Review the report, confirm status, and process payment according to the agreement. |
| End of Month 3 | Prepare for the [midpoint review](templates/month-3-review.md). | Decide together to continue, adapt, pause, or conclude the fellowship. |
| Months 5–6 | Build a [graduation portfolio](templates/graduation-portfolio.md) and name a next step. | Support a warm introduction, reference, grant plan, or alumni handoff where appropriate. |

## What good progress looks like

Progress is evidence, not a quota.

- Developer evidence includes a reproducible branch, tests, benchmarks, issue analysis, design discussion, reviews, documentation, or an upstream pull request. Upstream merge timing is outside a fellow’s control.
- Educator evidence includes a delivered session, attendance and return patterns, participant learning or contribution milestones, feedback, and reusable teaching material. Do not collect more attendee data than you need.

Use the [evaluation rubric](shared/evaluation-rubric.md) to make expectations visible. It is a conversation aid, not a surprise scorecard.

## When something changes

Tell your mentor or program lead as soon as you know. You never need to solve a difficult situation alone before asking for help.

- **Blocked:** include what you tried, the smallest question you need answered, and the next thing you will do if there is no response.
- **Behind plan:** adjust the scope before adding hours. Honest reporting is good standing.
- **Need time away:** a fellowship can pause for up to two months without penalty. See [payment and reporting](shared/payment-and-reporting.md).
- **Want to change project or format:** write down why the old plan no longer fits, the proposed replacement, and its next 30-day milestone. Confirm the change before treating it as agreed.
- **Need private reporting:** ask. Public proof of work is useful, but personal safety, payment data, and participant privacy come first.

## Working well in public

Be kind, precise, and patient—especially in unfamiliar technical communities. Read local contribution guidelines, distinguish facts from guesses, credit collaborators, and keep a durable record of decisions. Follow the [Code of Conduct](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) in all fellowship spaces and activities.

You own your work and your career. The fellowship can create structure and introductions; it cannot guarantee an upstream merge, grant, job, or residency.
